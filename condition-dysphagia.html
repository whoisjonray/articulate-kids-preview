Conditions we treat

Dysphagia

Eating, drinking and swallowing difficulties

When mealtimes are hard, it isn't always fussiness. Coughing, gagging, or refusing food can be signs your child finds it difficult to chew or swallow safely. That's dysphagia, and it's something we can help with.

The signs

Signs that eating or drinking might be difficult.

You know your child best. If mealtimes feel stressful, or a few of these are part of daily life, it's worth a proper look.

Coughing, gagging or choking at meals Effortful or very slow chewing A wet or gurgly voice after eating or drinking Drooling or dribbling Holding food in their cheeks Refusing certain textures Watering eyes or a red face while eating Frequent chest infections Distress or fear around mealtimes Slow weight gain or growth

Some children arrive with a diagnosis. Others just have parents who sense mealtimes shouldn't be this hard. Both are welcome, and you don't need a referral.

Why it matters

Safe swallowing is about more than food.

Swallowing is one of the most complex things the body does, and it has to happen dozens of times a meal. When it doesn't work smoothly, food or drink can go the wrong way, mealtimes become frightening, and a child can slowly take in less than they need to grow.

So a swallowing difficulty is clinical, not behavioural. It isn't your child being difficult, and it usually isn't something they'll simply grow out of on their own. The good news is that it responds well to the right assessment and support, and to a calmer plan for mealtimes at home.
