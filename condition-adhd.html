Conditions we treat

ADHD

ADHD is about far more than attention. For many children it shows up in how they listen, process what's said, and find their words. We look at attention and communication together — because for a lot of children, they're the same story.

The signs

What ADHD can look like at home and at school.

Every child is different, and you don't need a diagnosis to come to us. If a few of these feel familiar, that's reason enough to talk.

Always on the go, can't sit still Easily distracted, the smallest thing pulls them away Hyper one minute, zoned out the next Struggles to follow spoken instructions Talks over others or blurts things out Forgets what was just said Finds it hard to attend and listen, even one-to-one Big emotional ups and downs Words don't keep up with everything they want to say Homework and handwriting are a real effort

Some families arrive with an ADHD diagnosis. Others are still waiting, or simply feel something isn't quite adding up. Both are welcome here.

Why we're different

Attention and language are wired together.

A child who can't hold their attention often can't hold the thread of a sentence either. They lose the middle of an instruction. They know what they mean, but the words scatter before they arrive. So the "not listening" and the "not talking properly" turn out to be the same difficulty, seen from two sides.

Most clinics look at one piece at a time. We look at how it all fits together — attention, language, the senses, and the brain and gut underneath. Quite often, that's where the thing everyone else missed turns out to have been.

"Every child makes sense, once you know where to look."
